My Story

I was the calmest person in every room. Then August 2024 happened.

I've always been logical. Introverted. Unshakable. While others panicked, I stayed calm — I just couldn't relate to anxiety. Three jobs, 15-hour cognitive days, and I thought I was fine.

Then one afternoon I drank tea, ate a small cupcake, went for a jog — and nearly passed out. That was it. One moment became a permanent rewiring.

"I kept telling my brain to relax, be logical. It wouldn't work. My heartbeat would race. Breathing got hard. I started carrying crackers and water everywhere."
— Josh, Backend Developer & Founder

Now I'm also expecting a child, building a house, and planning a wedding — fighting anxiety quietly, alone, while trying to stay the provider everyone needs.

I reduced my workload and salary just to heal. This project is my path back — and I want it to help others walking the same road.

What Anxiety Really Feels Like
Random nervousness with no trigger — even just forgetting a name
Racing heartbeat and difficulty breathing, especially on an empty stomach
Fear of losing control — carrying inhalers as "insurance" just to feel safe
Waking up nervous, first reaching for the inhaler before anything else
Fighting it silently while no one around you knows
Still showing up — still building, still loving, still trying

The medication problem

Anxiety and mental health drugs in the Philippines are expensive. Many people who need them simply can't afford them. A portion of every donation after Phase 1 goes directly toward funding medication and mental health access for those who need it most.

Where Your Money Goes

Two phases. One honest mission.

This project has a clear two-part purpose. First, it funds the platform itself. Then, it becomes a vehicle for real mental health access.

🛠️

Phase 1: Build the Platform

The first ₱100K funds the development, hosting, and infrastructure of this project — a real product built by a real developer who needed to slow down to survive.

🏥

Phase 2: Fund Mental Health

After launch, donations are forwarded to mental hospitals and individuals who cannot afford anxiety and depression medication. Drugs are expensive. Access shouldn't be.

🫂

Share the Story

Every contribution helps destigmatize anxiety, especially among high-performing professionals who suffer quietly. You're not alone — and neither is anyone who finds this page.
